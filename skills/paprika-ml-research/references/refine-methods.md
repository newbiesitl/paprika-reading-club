# Refine methods from research

Start with the decision the evidence might improve. For a folder of related papers, infer candidate questions from the supplied focus and confirm only ambiguities that matter. A category can hold useful leads before it yields a reusable method.

## Inspect and compare

Select primary sources and research artifacts relevant to that question. Prefer mechanism-level reading over headline similarity. Inspect enough material to support each consequential claim; record unavailable details rather than filling them from memory. Search beyond the supplied set when a missing comparator or counterexample matters to the task.

Build a compact evidence comparison when helpful:

| Source/work and locator | Mechanism and conditions | Comparison and result | Limits or contradictions | Decision implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Separate what the authors report from what the comparison actually isolates. Check whether changed data, compute, architecture, reward, tuning budget, evaluation protocol, or access to external tools could explain the gain. A result with several changes may justify a combined-system choice without identifying the cause of improvement. Include useful null/negative results and publication/access gaps.

## Form a transferable rule

Extract the smallest conditional rule that changes a decision. Name the mechanism, prerequisites, plausible alternative, and evidence boundary. Look for multiple independent observations when available; a single study may support a narrowly scoped proposal, but repeated commentary on it adds no replication.

Example form: "When candidate actions can be evaluated against a trustworthy feedback signal within the available budget, compare feedback-driven improvement with the existing supervised baseline. Test whether improvement survives equal resource accounting and held-out evaluation." This is a proposed decision procedure, not a universal claim that a particular training method wins.

For transfer across domains, map the units of observation, action, outcome, feedback quality, horizon, observability, data collection, and resource costs. Mark mismatches explicitly. Analogy suggests a hypothesis; it does not establish efficacy in the new setting.

Write or revise a method record using the [library contract](method-library.md). Preserve existing valid evidence, contrary findings, and unresolved uncertainty. Do not mark scientific review complete because the source's identity was verified or the document passed a formatting check.

## Review, use, and revise

Where independent review is part of the project workflow, prepare the exact candidate and evidence for a different reviewer or the human. Continue useful provisional decision support while labeling review limits; do not claim final scientific acceptance. Send a review request only within existing communication authorization.

After a real experiment, compare the observed result with the earlier prediction and protocol. Distinguish a failed implementation, inadequate test power, broken assumptions, and evidence against the method. Narrow applicability or revise confidence accordingly. One positive result does not establish broad transfer; one inconclusive result does not falsify every version of the idea.

Record a revision and update the index when maintaining the library is authorized. Keep old decision records tied to the versions they actually used. Retract or supersede unsupported rules explicitly rather than silently editing away their history.
