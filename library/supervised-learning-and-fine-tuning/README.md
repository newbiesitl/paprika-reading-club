# Supervised Learning and Fine-Tuning

A human-requested category for studying what supervised learning can acquire, how training examples and sampling affect learnability, and how fine-tuning interacts with generalization and forgetting.

## Starting paper

[Finetuning with Sampling: SFT Learns Better Than You Think](arxiv-2610.02140.md) — Aayush Karan, Sitan Chen, and Yilun Du (2026).

The proposed reading lens is to separate the effects of the objective, the data distribution, and total computation. The paper changes the training traces before applying SFT, making it a useful comparison point for the club's [learning-beyond-imitation direction](../../methodology/core-ideology.md#1-learn-beyond-imitation).

## Proposed questions

- Does a result depend on the learning rule, the information available in the data, or which trajectories the learner can use?
- Do gains transfer to new tasks while retaining existing abilities?
- Are sampling, filtering, teacher information, and optimization costs all counted?
- What evidence would change our expectations about when SFT, RL, or a combination is appropriate?

The human selected the research topic and starting paper. Interpretations and conclusions remain open pending critical reading.
