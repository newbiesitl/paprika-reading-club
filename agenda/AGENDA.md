# Research agenda

The human has selected learning beyond imitation and world modeling as main research directions, with The Bitter Lesson as a methodological anchor. Supervised learning/fine-tuning and memory networks are also human-selected research focuses. The focused questions below remain proposed reading projects:

| Question | Starting material | Next useful step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When does a generative recommender become a useful behavioral world model? | OneRec | Separate action generation from response prediction and causal intervention |
| How should agents jointly learn to build and use tools? | SMITH | Inspect rewards, interface correctness, generalization, latency, and total cost |
| How far can simulated or judged user preferences support online decisions? | LLM judges and advertising literature intake | Separate screening, prediction, and causal lift; identify required live validation |
| When does shaping the training data make SFT a stronger learner? | [Finetuning with Sampling](../library/supervised-learning-and-fine-tuning/arxiv-2610.02140.md) | Separate objective, data support, compute, generalization, and forgetting |
| How can models retain and update useful experience within persistent memory? | [MemT-1 and related work](../library/memory-networks/README.md) | Compare state lifetime, update rules, retention, and total write/read cost |
| Which methods continue improving as compute, data, or interaction increases? | The Bitter Lesson | Compare a scalable baseline with a specialized alternative under explicit budgets |

The [core ideology](../methodology/core-ideology.md) adds AlphaZero, learned byte hierarchies, MuZero, and DreamerV3 as a connected reading path. Music/audio references remain available in the library. The Research Director chooses the first focused reading cycle.
